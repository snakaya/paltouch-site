When to Go to Japan if You Are Going to Shop

Written in Japan. Dates that change what is on the shelves, what it costs, and how full the shops are.

The date that matters most right now

1 November 2026. Tax-free shopping changes from a discount taken at the till to a refund collected when you leave the country. The sealed bag rule goes at the same time, which means you can open and use what you buy before you fly home. If your trip straddles that date, expect the process at the till to be unfamiliar to staff for a few weeks.

The threshold is the part people get wrong: it is 5,500 yen in one shop on the same day, so splitting the same list across two visits throws the exemption away. At the till, large shops have a separate tax-free desk while drugstores usually process it at any register, and the slip they staple into your passport is collected when you leave the country. The tax-free section covers what is and is not eligible.

Weeks to avoid

  • New Year, roughly 29 December to 4 January. Many independent shops close entirely. Hotel prices peak. Department stores open late and sell fukubukuro lucky bags, which is either the reason to come or a reason to stay away.
  • Golden Week, late April to early May. The whole country travels at once. Rooms cost roughly double and the trains are full.
  • Obon, mid-August. Similar, plus heat and humidity that make carrying shopping unpleasant.

Quiet and cheap

  • Early December. Winter packaging is already on the shelves, the New Year rush has not started, and rooms are at their cheapest.
  • Late January and February. The winter sales run through January. Cold, but the shops are empty on weekdays.
  • Late September and early October. Between the summer holidays and the autumn colours. Comfortable weather and normal prices.

The pattern worth planning around: a single holiday mid-week barely registers, but anything that joins a weekend moves the whole country. The quiet windows above are the mirror image, and they are the ones where the cheaper rooms are still available. Sales

Department stores run two main sale seasons, from the start of January and from the start of July. Drugstores and 100-yen shops do not really discount; their prices are the same all year, which is part of why they are worth the suitcase space.

Seasonal packaging

Snack flavours and stationery designs rotate roughly every six weeks, and regional editions do not travel between prefectures. If you see something you want, buy it there rather than planning to find it again at the airport.

Booking

Hotel prices in Japan move with demand rather than with how far ahead you book, so there is little advantage to booking a year out. The exception is the weeks listed above, where the cheaper rooms genuinely do sell out. Rakuten Travel shows the domestic rate in yen, which is the number to compare against.
