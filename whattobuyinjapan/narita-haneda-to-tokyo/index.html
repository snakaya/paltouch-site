Narita or Haneda to Your Tokyo Hotel: Skyliner, N'EX, Bus or Taxi

You clear immigration, collect a suitcase, and then have about four minutes before someone asks which way into Tokyo. This page answers that question for both airports, matched to the four neighbourhoods most of our readers base themselves in: Ueno, Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, and Ginza or Tokyo Station.

The short version: from Narita, the Keisei Skyliner is the fastest and cheapest fixed option if your hotel is near Ueno or on the JR Yamanote Line; from Haneda, the Tokyo Monorail or Keikyu Line both get you into central Tokyo in well under an hour for a few hundred yen. Read on for why, and for what each option actually costs.

Narita sits about 60–70 km from central Tokyo. Every option below leaves from the arrivals level at Terminal 1, 2 or 3.

A cheaper Keisei option exists if the Skyliner's price is the issue: the Access Express runs the same Narita–Nippori–Ueno route on ordinary Keisei tracks for 1,240 yen ($8), about half the Skyliner fare, in exchange for a slower, non-reserved ride.

Narita: which one for which base

Ueno

The easy case. The Keisei Skyliner runs straight to Keisei-Ueno Station in 40–41 minutes, no transfer, and Ueno is the one base on this page the Skyliner was practically built for. Buy the ticket online in advance and you walk off the plane and onto the platform.

Fix the Skyliner seat before you land

The Skyliner is a reserved-seat train. Buying online in advance fixes the yen price and lets you walk from arrivals to the platform without the ticket-counter queue.

Shinjuku

Two direct options. The Narita Express is the only train that reaches Shinjuku Station without a transfer, in a seat with a dedicated luggage bay, for 5,200 yen on the round-trip ticket. The Skyliner plus a Yamanote Line change at Nippori is the cheaper route if you are travelling light enough for one platform change. The Airport Limousine Bus also runs direct to Shinjuku hotels for 3,600 yen, which is worth it if your hotel is one of the bus's actual stops.

Ikebukuro

Same logic as Shinjuku: Skyliner to Nippori then the Yamanote Line is the budget route, the Airport Limousine Bus (3,100–3,600 yen) is the one that puts you at the hotel door without changing trains, and N'EX gets you to Tokyo Station or Shinjuku for the same onward change.

Ginza or Tokyo Station

The Narita Express runs directly into Tokyo Station in as little as 53 minutes and is the fastest option on this list for this base. The Airport Limousine Bus to Tokyo Station (3,100 yen) is the direct alternative if you would rather not carry bags through a station transfer. For Ginza itself, walk from Tokyo Station or take a taxi for the last stretch.

Haneda: which one for which base

Ueno

No single-change option is as direct as the Narita Skyliner here. Take the Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho (about 20 minutes, 520 yen) or the Keikyu Line to Shinagawa, then change to the JR Yamanote Line for Ueno. The fixed-fare taxi (9,100 yen) is the one-step alternative if you are travelling with a group and splitting the fare.

Shinjuku

The Keikyu Line runs through to Shinjuku in about 30 minutes without you needing to plan the transfer yourself. The limousine bus from Haneda is the direct-to-hotel option for 1,400 yen if your hotel is one of its stops.

Ikebukuro

Keikyu Line through-service again, about 38 minutes from Terminal 3. The limousine bus also serves Ikebukuro directly for 1,400 yen; the fixed-fare taxi here is the most expensive of the four bases at 11,200 yen, reflecting the longer distance.

Ginza or Tokyo Station

The Tokyo Monorail to Hamamatsucho or the Keikyu Line to Tokyo Station (about 20 minutes either way) are the fastest routes in. For a taxi, ask for the fixed fare to Chiyoda ward (7,600 yen) if your hotel is near Tokyo Station; Ginza itself sits in Chuo ward, which is outside Haneda's fixed-fare zone, so that leg runs on the meter.

Buy in advance or at the counter

The trains with reserved or limited seating are worth locking in before you fly: the Keisei Skyliner and the Narita Express both sell out individual departures around flight-heavy hours, and buying online fixes the yen price and skips the ticket counter queue right after you land. The limousine bus from Haneda and Narita's Airport Limousine Bus can usually be bought at the counter or vending machine on the day — several routes explicitly allow boarding without a reservation — so there is less to gain by prebooking unless you want a specific departure locked in.

Taxis need no advance booking at all from either airport; you simply queue at the rank and ask for the fixed fare. The one thing worth sorting before you land is connectivity and a transit card: a a Japan eSIM activates from a QR code before the plane touches down, so you can check live timetables the moment you reach the platform, and a Tokyo Subway Ticket or an IC card covers every onward train and bus for the rest of the trip.
